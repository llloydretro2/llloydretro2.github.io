Employer-Side Matching Architecture
  • Designed a three-layer architecture for job-to-candidate matching — hard filtering, coarse retrieval over a whole-profile embedding, then per-field cosine scoring with weighted summation — in which both sides decompose into field blocks under a shared template and the ranking layer emits a per-field score profile rather than a scalar, rendering every ranking decision attributable to the passage that produced it.
  • Established per-field standardization as a precondition for interpretable weighting, since a field's influence on the ordering scales with the product of its weight and its own dispersion; statistics are estimated offline over the full library, preserving comparability across queries and admitting incremental extension of a result set.
  • Rejected rank-reciprocal fusion within the ranking layer on the grounds that its premise — combining scorers without a common scale — does not obtain there, and that rank substitution discards effect magnitude.
  • Formalized missing-value semantics as renormalization over observed fields rather than zero-imputation, distinguishing absence of requirement on the posting side from absence of evidence on the candidate side.
  • Constrained the filtering stage to predicates expressible within the retrieval query, separating non-negotiable qualifications from relaxable ranges, and excluded health and physical-condition attributes from the schema as a compliance boundary.
  • Specified the resulting score matrix as the interface for two downstream uses: attribution of employer preference into weight adjustments, and per-candidate gap identification for targeted follow-up.
Multimodal Assessment of Asynchronous Interviews
  • Designed and implemented a staged pipeline reducing a single recording — camera and screen capture, without operating-system instrumentation — to timestamped structured records in which every assertion carries a verifiable source in the transcript or an identified frame.
  • Adopted dual-granularity segmentation on the observation that the resolution at which a citation is verifiable differs from that at which a description is coherent, and specified requirement coverage, citation verifiability, and event-to-frame traceability as the evaluation criteria the pipeline would have to meet.
  • Bounded the system's claim to verifying consistency between assertion and demonstrated behavior rather than grading performance, treating model judgments of fine-grained quality as unvalidated against human annotation and therefore outside what the system asserts.
  • Surveyed the legal and adversarial boundary of the approach: provider constraints on workplace affect inference, biometric-privacy exposure, and prompt injection delivered through shared screen content.
Controlled Study of the Retrieval Embedding Layer
  • Conducted controlled ablations, each with a length- and format-matched control arm, to determine which structured fields carry signal in the indexed representation; a rule-based extractor recovered most of the benefit of an LLM-based one without additional latency.
  • Demonstrated that query and document embeddings drawn from different models cannot be mixed — retrieval degrades to chance while surface indicators remain nominal — establishing atomicity as a requirement for model migration.
